# Podu Roș evidence for a V3 scenario

Checked 17 October 2026. This file distinguishes observations from assumptions for the planned Nicolina–Palat morning run.

| Topic | Available evidence | Use in project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local vehicle trajectories | [Cleju and Catargiu (2026)](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26103027) filmed one northern Podu Roș view on 16 July 2024, 19:09–21:55. It retained 5,378 complete car trajectories. The [authors' release](https://trafalert-project.gitlab.io/TrafAlert/papers/trajoutlierdet.html) provides detections and selected IDs. | Evening route shape and relative camera-window activity only. It excludes incomplete tracks and other classes; it does not count all junction arrivals. See [source preparation](README.md). |
| Daily traffic shape | [Iași PMUD 2023](https://vision4iasi.ro/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/PMUD-Iasi-FINAL.pdf), pages 89–90, lists the Podu Roș junction among three May 2021 24-hour count sites. Its published hourly chart combines the sites. | Optional broad time-of-day shape, labelled as a three-site proxy. No site-specific hourly or turning table was obtained. See [PMUD extraction](../iasi-pmud-2023/README.md). |
| Morning Nicolina → Palat and reverse | User-specified direction; no morning local turning counts in the available sources. | Scenario assumption. State both rates explicitly. Do not call the chosen split measured. |
| Speed and spacing | The local paper studies short trajectory shapes, not a reported mean travel speed or car-to-car gap. An exploratory estimate from its released car detections is described below. The map stores SUMO lane speed limits; SUMO car-following has a minimum standing gap and time-headway parameter. | Keep the exploratory evening camera estimate, modeled limits, and simulated values distinct. No defensible local morning mean speed or observed average gap is established. |
| Pedestrian crossings | The retained OSM input contains crossing and sidewalk tags. OSM markings are map evidence, not pedestrian volumes or verified signal timing. |

| Historical scale | [A 2002 air-pollution study](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/seikisho/39/1%2C2/39_1%2C2_47/_pdf), Table 1, lists Podu Roș among sites with roughly 16,000–30,000 vehicles/day. | Historical context only: a wide, old site category without current approach/turn counts. Proposals are not evidence that new geometry already exists. |

The existing `podu-ros-commute` package is a version 1 estimated directional scenario. Its 2.4 versus 0.35 peak multipliers and 1,943.855422 selected camera tracks/hour reference are preparation choices, not morning Nicolina–Palat measurements. See its [README](../../../../experiments/drafts/podu-ros-commute/README.md).

The retained OSM source has 30 `highway=crossing` nodes inside the conversion box. The existing SUMO road network has no crossing or walking-area edges. Re-running SUMO 1.28.0 `netconvert` from the OSM source with `--osm.sidewalks --osm.crossings` segfaulted in this environment. Converting the existing road network with `--sidewalks.guess --crossings.guess` succeeded and produced 73 crossing and 126 walking-area edges. Those are **inferred** crossings and need site review; they are not an import of the 30 tagged OSM crossings. Two selected walks were checked to traverse crossing edges in SUMO's FCD output.

The V3 candidate map has 190 passenger-permitted ordinary lanes. Its encoded speed limits are 30–50 km/h (median 50 km/h), not observed mean speeds. Balthasar writes the V3 car type without overrides, so [SUMO's documented passenger defaults](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Definition_of_Vehicles,_Vehicle_Types,_and_Routes.html) apply: 5 m vehicle length, 2.5 m minimum standing gap, and 1 s desired time headway. None is an observed average gap between moving cars.

An [exploratory estimate](visible-speed-estimate.json) derived with [the reproducible script](estimate_visible_speed.py) projects the authors' selected detections using their camera calibration and takes centered position differences every 25 detections. Among 47,497 usable evening camera-time samples, the mean is **19.26 km/h**, the median is **20.62 km/h**, and 28.7% of samples are at or below 0.5 m/s. The mean of 5,377 per-track means is **27.45 km/h**; that difference reflects how time samples weight vehicles that remain visible longer. These are our estimates, not figures reported by the authors or validated ground-truth speeds. Projection, tracking, processing timestamps, and the single-view evening selection limit their use. They are not morning or full-intersection speeds and are not used to calibrate this V3 scenario.

The trained project's [recorded Balthasar run](../../../../experiments/drafts/podu-ros-neural/evaluation.json) sampled a **36.83 km/h** ordinary-lane mean speed and **43.89 m** mean same-lane adjacent bumper separation over ten snapshots in 1,000 simulated seconds. These are **simulation outputs**, not street measurements. The gap calculation subtracts the assumed 5 m vehicle length from front-position differences.
